Mini splits exist for the rooms central air forgets. The garage workshop, the sunroom that bakes by noon, the addition the ducts never reached, the Cheyenne house that was built without ductwork at all: each of these can be heated and cooled on its own by one or more indoor heads. A slim line set carries refrigerant, power, and condensate drainage through a single three-inch wall penetration to the outdoor unit, so there is no duct system losing energy along the way. The inverter compressor speeds up and slows down with the load rather than cycling hard, which keeps the room steady, quiet, and cheap to run. In winter, the same equipment works as a cold-climate heat pump, not a cooling unit pretending to heat.

Design is where mini split projects succeed or fail, and Cheyenne HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because a head too big for the room cools fast but leaves the air clammy. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched sensibly across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and head placement and style affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk every room with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, hidden where they can be and tidy where they cannot.

Installation gets the same rigor as our largest systems. The line set is pressure-tested with nitrogen and evacuated to a micron-gauge-verified vacuum, and flare connections are torqued and leak-checked, since a weeping flare is the classic mini split failure. Condensate is drained or pumped reliably, and the charge is adjusted for line set length per the manufacturer's tables. We commission every zone in both heating and cooling, then hand you the remotes with the settings explained in plain terms. Why Hire a Professional for Ductless Mini Split Installation?

Mini splits look simple hanging on the wall, and that illusion produces a steady stream of failed installs. The flare connections that join the line set are the system's weak point: under-torqued or badly formed flares weep refrigerant for months, and the first symptom is a head blowing room-temperature air a season later. Skipping the nitrogen pressure test and micron-verified vacuum leaves moisture in a circuit that inverter compressors handle badly. Line sets run past the manufacturer's length and lift tables quietly cost capacity, and a condensate drain without proper fall stains a wall the first humid week.

Sizing discipline matters even more here than with central systems, because the smallest available heads are already plenty for many bedrooms; the wrong pick short-cycles, leaves humidity behind, and never settles. Our experienced technicians size each zone honestly, torque and leak-test every flare, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, set charge adjustments to the line length, and commission each head in both modes. Refrigerant work is federally regulated, and the electrical side needs a properly sized circuit and disconnect. The result is what a mini split should be: silent, efficient, and boring for fifteen years, instead of a mysterious underperformer from a rushed weekend install.

Maintenance Tips for Ductless Mini Split Installation

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Clear snow, leaves, and clippings away from the outdoor unit year-round
  • Do not wait for a musty smell; get the blower wheel and coil cleaned by a pro on a regular basis
  • On humid summer days, confirm the drain line outside is dripping as it should
  • Choose a setpoint and leave it alone, since inverters run best without constant on and off switching
  • Rinse or wash the indoor unit's filter screens monthly; they load up faster than furnace filters
  • Schedule service once a year so charge, flares, and drainage get verified before small issues grow
  • In winter, expect defrost pauses and light steam from the outdoor unit; solid ice means call us
